AUTOBRIQ · bench test

Testing the YF-S201 flow sensor

Just the sensor, the ESP32 and one 10k resistor. No valve, no hose: blow through the sensor and watch the litres count. Uses the actuator-test sketch from commit 18efb7e or later.

Don't wire yellow straight to a 5 V pull-up into the ESP32 with no resistor

The sensor's signal swings to 5 V and the ESP32 pin is rated for 3.3 V. The 10k in series keeps the current into the pin tiny, about 0.1 mA, which is safe. Don't add a pull-up from GPIO34 to 3V3 with it: the two make a divider and no pulse ever counts.

Test

Run it

  1. Flash the sketch

    • Close any serial monitor first, then run:
    pio run -e actuator-test -t upload

  2. Open the serial monitor

    pio device monitor
    Ctrl+C quits it. Close it again before the next flash.

  3. Blow through the sensor

    • No button needed. Blow in the direction of the arrow on its body, or pour water through it.
    • Watch the FLOW box at the top right of the Nextion, e.g. FLOW 0.03 L. It is always on screen and updates once a second.
    • The phone page shows it after the status, and the serial monitor prints FLOW TEST 0.03 L (14 pulses) each second pulses arrive.
    Pass: the FLOW number goes up while air or water moves and holds when it stops.

  4. Optional: count one fill

    • Tap WATER ON (R6), or press 6, to count from zero for one run. Relay 6 clicks; with no valve connected that's harmless.
    • Tap again or STOP ALL. The serial monitor prints that run's total, e.g. Water: 0.05 L.
    • Reset the ESP32 (EN button) to zero the FLOW TEST total.

Calibration

Check the 450 pulses per litre with water

Run water through the sensor into a measuring jug and stop at 1 L on the jug. Compare with the litres on the screen. If they differ, change PULSES_PER_L near the top of actuator-test/actuator_test.cpp:

PULSES_PER_L = 450 × screen litres ÷ jug litres

Example: the screen says 1.10 L when the jug has 1.00 L, so 450 × 1.10 = 495. Do it two or three times and average; the flow rate changes the reading slightly.
