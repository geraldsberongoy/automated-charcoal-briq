AUTOBRIQ · control board

Universal PCB layout for the ESP32 control side

One perfboard carries the ESP32 and does all the 5 V / 3.3 V / GND distribution. Every module plugs into a labeled pin with a Dupont jumper. Every signal runs straight across its own row, so no wires cross. Matches the actuator-test pin map.

Relay map note

The relay channels in this guide are the bench rig as wired (CH5 mixer, CH6 water valve). The thesis assigns CH5 to the water valve and CH6 to the 30 A automotive relay for the mixer. See docs/PROJECT_CONTEXT.md in the repository. Swap two signal wires and two GPIO entries before the final build if the thesis map is kept.

Low voltage only on this board

Only 5 V, 3.3 V and the signal wires go on the perfboard. The 12 V side (EcoFlow, E-stop, relay contacts, actuators, valve) and the mixer's AC-DC supply stay on the relay board's screw terminals, as in the wiring guide. Never bring 12 V onto this board.

Whole system

Every component and every wire

The perfboard sits in the middle. Left of it is the 12 V side (EcoFlow, E-stop, relay contacts, actuators, valve), bottom is the mixer's own AC supply, right is the 5 V side (EcoFlow USB-A 5 V, Nextion, HX711 and load cell, flow sensor). A dot is a joint; lines that cross without a dot are not connected.

Fuse: an inline blade or glass fuse holder, 10 A, as close to the EcoFlow + as you can, so a short anywhere downstream blows the fuse instead of melting wire. The 5 V side comes from the EcoFlow's USB-A port straight into the perfboard and stays there: it doesn't need to join the 12 V GND bus, because the relay contacts isolate the 12 V side and no signal crosses between them. The E-stop doesn't cut the USB, so the screen and ESP32 stay on and show STOPPED. The mixer's AC-DC supply never touches these buses.

Layout

Component side, seen from above

Grid = 2.54 mm holes. The board needs 38 × 27 holes (about 10 × 7 cm), so a 7 × 10 cm board or bigger. When you flip it to solder, everything is mirrored left-right. Keep this picture next to you the right way up and check each joint against it.

5 V 3.3 V GND relay inputs HX711 ESP TX2 → Nextion RX Nextion TX → ESP RX2 flow sensor Thick bars = power rails (bare wire on the solder side, every pin on it joined). Small black squares = male header pins for Dupont jumpers.

Check your ESP32 before soldering the headers: most DOIT DevKit V1 boards have their pin rows 10 holes (25.4 mm) apart, as drawn. Some clones are 9 holes. Plug the ESP32 into the two loose female headers first, push them into the board, and solder them where they fit.

Recommended extra: a 10 kΩ from each relay IN pin to VCC, soldered on the relay board's input header. It holds the relays off while the ESP32 pins float during flashing or reset, which is what was breaking the uploads.

Build

Build and check order

  1. Fit the ESP32 headers

    • Push both female headers onto the ESP32's pins, then into the board at the spot shown, antenna toward the top edge.
    • Solder one corner pin of each, check it sits flat, then the rest. Unplug the ESP32 before soldering anything else.

  2. Power: terminal, rails, C1

    • Solder the screw terminal, run the 5V, GND and 3V3 rails as bare wire on the solder side, then the power pin strips on top of them.
    • C1: the striped (−) side goes to GND.

  3. ESP32 power pins

    • VIN to the 5V rail, left GND to the GND rail, 3V3 to the 3V3 rail.

  4. Signals

    • One wire per row, from the ESP32 header pin straight to its labeled edge pin. R1 goes in the D13 row.

  5. Meter check, ESP32 out

    • Ohms between 5V and GND, then 3V3 and GND: no short. (C1 makes the meter count up for a moment; that's fine.)
    • Beep test each signal: ESP32 header pin to its edge pin, and no beep to its neighbors.
    Pass: no shorts, every labeled pin beeps to the right ESP32 pin only.

  6. Power up, ESP32 still out

    • Connect the USB-A cable to the screw terminal and plug it into the EcoFlow.
    Pass: 5.0 V on the 5V pins and on the VIN socket hole. 3V3 pins read 0 V (the ESP32 makes the 3.3 V).

  7. ESP32 in, then modules

    • Power off, plug in the ESP32, power on: 3V3 pins now read 3.3 V.
    • Plug the modules in with the jumper tables above, one at a time, and run the bench test after each.
